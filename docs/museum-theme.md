# Museum theme: *The Curator*

A reskin of Grand Hotel as a fictional Victorian museum: **the Royal Museum of Antiquities, London, 1890**.
The rules and the bot stay exactly the same; only names, text, colours and art change. Every artefact and staff
card keeps the number of the card it replaces, so each one plays exactly like its hotel twin.

## The idea in one paragraph

You are a young curator. Artefacts arrive from expeditions, donors and auctions; you buy them, restore them on
your three workbenches with plaster, linen, varnish and gold leaf, and then put them on display in a gallery of
the right department. Fill whole wings for extra prestige, keep the Royal patron's favour for the three royal
visits, and hire the right staff. Most prestige after seven seasons wins.

### Palette and look

Museum-label typography over aged card stock: oxblood, museum green, brass and ink, with the four department
colours (terracotta red, sand gold, royal blue, museum green) on the card bands. Rooms become arched gallery
doorways and glass display cases. Portraits become objects on plinths, lit like Victorian museum photographs.

## Royal visits (Emperor tiles) and Trustee commissions (Politics)

Same effects, renamed: *“After season 3, 5 and 7 the Royal patron visits.”* Bonuses and penalties read the same
with guineas, prestige, galleries and materials swapped in (e.g. *“Lose all materials in your storeroom”*,
*“Free gallery on floor 1–2, already filled”*). Trustee commissions read *“Fill 2 complete floors”*,
*“4 Classical and 3 Egyptian galleries filled”* and so on.

## Wording to change in the interface

Table → **Museum floor**, Café → **Workshop**, Hotel → **Galleries**, Staff → **Staff**, Log → **Journal**;
“Invite” → **Acquire**, “Check in” → **Put on display**, “Serve” → **Apply from store**, “Empty table” →
**Empty bench**, “Your turn: invite a guest, then take a die” → **“Your turn: acquire an artefact, then take a die.”**
